## Decisions by group

### fasteners

- Function: Bolts, screws, nuts and washers for all bolted joints.
- Conventional: COTS (ISO bolts, nuts, washers). In-house AM: COTS (ISO bolts, nuts, washers).
- Alternatives considered: Custom-rolled IN718 bolts to NAS/MS drawings; studs with captive nuts.
- Why: ISO 4014/4017 bolts and ISO 4032 nuts in A286 or Inconel 718 are catalogue items. The joint sizing (ffsc/structures.py) only needs proof strength and size, so nothing here justifies a custom fastener.

### seals

- Function: Static seals at every bolted joint.
- Conventional: COTS metallic C-seal. In-house AM: COTS metallic C-seal.
- Alternatives considered: Spiral-wound gaskets; welded omega seals; elastomer O-rings (rejected, LOX and 820 K gas).
- Why: Metallic C-seals in Inconel with a soft plating cover cryogenic to 1,100 K service and the 400-920 bar range. Standard sizes are catalogue items; large diameters are quoted per order.

### instrumentation

- Function: Chamber and pump-discharge pressure transducers, turbopump speed pickups.
- Conventional: COTS transducer. In-house AM: COTS transducer.
- Alternatives considered: In-house strain-gauge transducers.
- Why: Flight-rated cryogenic transducers exist; building them in-house adds no value.

### valves

- Function: Main oxidiser, main fuel and the two preburner control valves with electromechanical actuators.
- Conventional: custom valve, COTS actuator parts. In-house AM: custom valve, COTS actuator parts.
- Alternatives considered: COTS cryogenic ball valves (rated to about 20 bar; Moog datasheet); small-bore 1,400 bar ball valves (1/4-3/4 in bore only).
- Why: No catalogue valve combines 700-920 bar with a 100-160 mm bore at cryogenic temperature, so all four valves are custom. Only the actuator motor, gearbox and position sensor are catalogue parts.

### bearings

- Function: Angular-contact rolling bearings, propellant-cooled.
- Conventional: COTS bearing, modified. In-house AM: COTS bearing, modified.
- Alternatives considered: Hybrid Si3N4 bearings (option C1); hydrostatic bearings (option C2).
- Why: Precision bearings are bought and modified (cage, clearance, coatings) for cryogenic service. The CH4 shaft sits near 2.5 million DN, at the edge of steel bearings; see options C1 and C2.

### hydrostatic_bearings

- Function: Propellant-fed hydrostatic journal and thrust bearings (I2, option C2).
- Conventional: machined from forging. In-house AM: machined from forging.
- Alternatives considered: Hybrid Si3N4 ball bearings (3.0 million DN limit, below the 3.1 million the CH4 shaft reaches at 10 bar inlet).
- Why: No rolling-element DN limit and no rolling contact; the cost is a bleed of a few percent of the CH4 pump flow from discharge to the bearing pockets. Machined in-house (orifices, pockets, coatings).

### laser_igniters

- Function: Laser ignition heads with sapphire window on both preburners (I2, option N2).
- Conventional: custom torch igniter. In-house AM: custom torch igniter.
- Alternatives considered: Spark-torch igniters with GOX/GCH4 taps (iteration 1); NRP plasma igniters (start only).
- Why: Laser breakdown gets easier as pressure rises and needs no propellant taps, valves or lines. The laser head and driver are bought; the window port is engine-specific.

### welds

- Function: Butt welds that replace flange pairs on joints that are never opened (I2, options I1 and I3).
- Conventional: bent tube / formed sheet, welded. In-house AM: bent tube / formed sheet, welded.
- Alternatives considered: Bolted flange pair with metallic C-seal (iteration 1).
- Why: Orbital TIG or EB weld with radiography; no flanges, bolts or seals, no leak path.

### igniters

- Function: Spark-torch igniters on both preburners (GOX/GCH4 torch).
- Conventional: custom torch igniter. In-house AM: custom torch igniter.
- Alternatives considered: Laser or plasma ignition at start (option N2); pyrotechnic igniters (single use, rejected for reuse).
- Why: A spark plug is COTS; the torch body, its valves and propellant taps are engine-specific.

### chamber_liner

- Function: Regeneratively cooled combustion chamber to eps 12 (liner, ribs, closeout).
- Conventional: forged billet, milled channels, electroformed closeout. In-house AM: L-PBF GRCop-42 liner, DED closeout.
- Alternatives considered: Milled CuCrZr or GRCop-84 liner with electroformed nickel closeout (Vulcain/RS-25 practice).
- Why: The 120 MW/m2 throat flux needs a copper alloy; GRCop-42 keeps its strength to about 900 K. L-PBF builds the 384-768 channel geometry and the bifurcation directly (PicoGK model). The milled route needs a forged copper billet, about 1,000 channel passes and an electroformed closeout.

### nozzle

- Function: Channel-wall regeneratively cooled nozzle, eps 12 to 34.
- Conventional: forged jacket, milled channels, brazed closeout. In-house AM: laser-wire DED.
- Alternatives considered: Brazed tube-wall nozzle (RS-25, J-2); radiation-cooled extension (not possible at sea level).
- Why: A 1.37 m channel-wall nozzle is too large for most powder-bed machines; laser-wire DED with integral channels (NASA RAMPT) is the AM route. The conventional route is a milled channel jacket with a brazed closeout.

### coolant_manifolds

- Function: Inlet, distribution, outlet and exit manifolds of the two coolant circuits.
- Conventional: bent tube / formed sheet, welded. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Printed integral with the jackets (option I2).
- Why: Welded ring manifolds are standard; printing them separately mainly saves weld inspection.

### jumpers

- Function: Coolant jumpers across the bolted eps-12 joint.
- Conventional: bent tube / formed sheet, welded. In-house AM: bent tube / formed sheet, welded.
- Alternatives considered: Removed by a one-piece bimetallic chamber and nozzle (option I5).
- Why: Short bent tubes with flanges; simple to fabricate either way.

### flanges

- Function: Flange pairs for ducts, jumpers and vehicle interfaces.
- Conventional: machined from forged ring. In-house AM: machined from forged ring.
- Alternatives considered: Butt welds (options I1, I3).
- Why: Machined from catalogue forged rings; the ring blank is COTS, the machining is to print.

### injector_single_piece

- Function: Main injector printed in one piece - fuel plenum, faceplate, 173 posts, interpropellant plate (I2, option I4).
- Conventional: L-PBF. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Machined forgings with 173 brazed posts (iteration 1).
- Why: 176 parts and 346 brazed or welded joints become one print; only the flange faces and seal grooves are machined.

### injector_structure

- Function: Main injector body and fuel plenum, faceplate, interpropellant plate and ox-rich gas dome.
- Conventional: machined from forging. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Single printed injector (option I4).
- Why: Forged IN718 bodies machined and welded are the heritage route; L-PBF removes most welds.

### injector_posts

- Function: 173 coaxial ox-rich gas posts.
- Conventional: turned posts, brazed in. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Printed with the injector body (option I4).
- Why: Swiss-turned posts are cheap to make; the cost is in 346 brazed joints and their inspection.

### tvc_lugs

- Function: TVC actuator attach lugs.
- Conventional: machined from forging. In-house AM: machined from forging.
- Alternatives considered: Printed with the dome.
- Why: Simple load-path parts; machining from forging is cheapest.

### gimbal

- Function: Two-axis gimbal block, yoke, base plate and pins.
- Conventional: machined from forging. In-house AM: machined from forging.
- Alternatives considered: Spherical bearing gimbal (higher friction under 2.7 MN).
- Why: Ti-6Al-4V forgings for the block and yoke, A286 pins; standard machining.

### turbine_rotor

- Function: Turbine blisks (disk and rotor blades).
- Conventional: vacuum-cast superalloy. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Forged disk with inserted SX blades ($15k per aero SX blade, 60-90 week lead time); AM blisk.
- Why: Integral blisks avoid 84-125 blade roots each. The ox-rich side needs a burn-resistant alloy (SX500 class); the fuel-rich side uses cast MAR-M-247 (SX blades are option C3).

### turbine_stator

- Function: Turbine nozzle rings.
- Conventional: vacuum-cast superalloy. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Brazed vane segments.
- Why: One cast or printed ring per turbine.

### hot_gas_housings

- Function: Turbine inlet housings and exhaust collectors.
- Conventional: vacuum-cast superalloy. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Sheet-metal fabricated collectors.
- Why: Large, thin, hot and complex; casting or printing beats fabrication.

### pump_housings

- Function: Pump volutes, inlet and bearing housings, kick stage, casing flange.
- Conventional: investment casting. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Machined from forging (heavier).
- Why: Volutes are classic investment castings; L-PBF lets the volute and diffuser merge.

### impellers_inducers

- Function: Shrouded centrifugal impellers and helical inducers.
- Conventional: machined from forging. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Hub and shroud machined separately and brazed.
- Why: A shrouded impeller cannot be machined in one piece; L-PBF builds it whole.

### shafts

- Function: Turbopump shafts.
- Conventional: machined from forging. In-house AM: machined from forging.
- Alternatives considered: none worth considering.
- Why: Forged Inconel 718, turned and ground; AM brings nothing here.

### preburners

- Function: Ox-rich and fuel-rich preburner shells, injector heads and ports.
- Conventional: machined from forging. In-house AM: L-PBF.
- Alternatives considered: Printed with the turbine inlet housing.
- Why: Heads have many small passages; printing removes drilling and plugging.

### ducts

- Function: Discharge, feed, return and cross-feed ducts.
- Conventional: bent tube / formed sheet, welded. In-house AM: bent tube / formed sheet, welded.
- Alternatives considered: Printed elbows (only where the bend radius is too tight).
- Why: Bent and welded IN718 tube; catalogue tube stock, fabrication to print.
